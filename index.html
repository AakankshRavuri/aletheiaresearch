In-Depth Projects

Empirical Research Projects

Detailed methodologies, mathematical models, interactive tools, and downloadable datasets for our three research projects. Click any project to explore its full page view.

Project 1 • Consumer Pricing Multi-Store Longitudinal Audit

Quantifying Package Size Reduction vs. Price Changes (Shrinkflation)

A multi-year study tracking 450 consumer products across 6 major retail grocery chains. By matching sticker prices with package weights and volume metrics, this project measures effective unit cost increases that headline inflation numbers miss. Includes an interactive unit loss calculator, full regression output tables, and dataset exports.

Items Tracked: 450 Unique SKUs
Average Weight Reduction: -8.2% per package
Effective Unit Price Increase: +14.3% per ounce/gram
Project 2 • Monetary Analysis Macroeconomic Time-Series Modeling

Federal Reserve Interest Rate Changes and Retail Price Adjustments

A time-series analysis measuring how fast interest rate changes made by the Federal Reserve move through the supply chain into store prices. Analyzed 60 monthly periods of economic data alongside retail price records, complete with a monetary policy lag simulation engine.

Transmission Lag: 6.5 to 9 Months
Data Window: 2021 – 2026 Monthly Data
Pass-Through Measure: β = 0.683
Project 3 • Spatial Economics Zip-Code Level Price Dispersion

Retail Price Differences Across Locations and Neighborhood Incomes

Collected and compared pricing data for identical household goods across 18 zip codes, examining price differences across store locations, income areas, and market concentration levels. Features an interactive regional price variance estimator.

Locations Sampled: 18 Zip Codes Audited
Price Spread (Standard Dev): 14.2% Variance
Lower-Income Zip Premium: +8.6% Average Difference

RESEARCH BRIEF 01 PUBLISHED Q2 2024

Consumer Purchasing Power & Real Wage Trends Post-2021

An extensive study comparing pay increases to real buying power. Demonstrates how package size changes create unmeasured inflation that significantly erodes household purchasing power beyond standard CPI metrics.

RESEARCH BRIEF 02 PUBLISHED Q4 2024

Examining Inflation Measurement Methods and Quality Adjustments

A deep examination into how official statistical agencies apply hedonic quality adjustments to inflation figures, analyzing structural gaps when measuring rapid consumer packaged goods downsizing.

RESEARCH BRIEF 03 PUBLISHED Q2 2025

Interest Rate Changes and Time Lags in Consumer Markets

Empirical evaluation tracking central bank policy transmission across commercial financing, supply chains, and eventual store shelf price adjustments over a 60-month dataset.

RESEARCH BRIEF 04 PUBLISHED Q1 2026

Price Changes in Grocery Retail: Upward Flexibility vs. Downward Stickiness

An econometric study of asymmetric price transmission in grocery retail, showing how shelf prices rise quickly during input inflation but drop slowly during wholesale price deflation.

Data Collection and Processing Steps

To ensure high data quality, our group implemented a three-step process to record, clean, and standardize pricing information from physical stores and online listings.

STEP 1: Collection & Verification
Recording product prices, net package weight/volume, and brand information directly from shelf tags and verified online store listings.
STEP 2: Unit Standardizing
Converting prices to standard units ($ per ounce, $ per gram, or $ per count) to calculate real price changes regardless of package size modifications.
STEP 3: Economic Data Integration
Combining retail price records with macroeconomic data from the St. Louis Federal Reserve (FRED) to compare against CPI and interest rate trends.
